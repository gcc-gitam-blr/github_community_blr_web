# Charter of the GitHub Community Club, GITAM Bengaluru

> **Draft for the club to review.** Nothing here is in force until the core team agrees to it at a meeting and two
> admins publish it under `/admin` → **Policies** (one writes the version, a different one publishes it). Lines marked
> **To decide** are open questions, not rules. Edit freely; this file is only the starting point.

## 1. Who we are

The GitHub Community Club BLR is a student-run club at GITAM University Bengaluru. We teach Git, GitHub and open
source by doing them: real repositories, real pull requests, real reviews. We are not part of GitHub, Inc. and do not
speak for it.

Our aim for every member is simple: **a first merged pull request in their first term, and a reason to make a second.**

## 2. What we do

- **Sessions and workshops** through the year, starting with a beginner session on making a first contribution.
- **Epoch**, our flagship technical event in December.
- **Club projects** that members build together in the club's GitHub organisation, `gcc-gitam-blr`.
- **The website** at the club's domain, which is itself one of our projects and open to contributions.

Every event is open to beginners. Nobody needs to know how to code to come along.

## 3. Members

1. Any student of GITAM Bengaluru can join. It is free.
2. Joining takes a name and an email. A GitHub username and interests can be added later.
3. A member can leave at any time with the unsubscribe link in any club email, or by writing to the club email.
   Their details are deleted when they ask, as the privacy page says.
4. Members get the club's emails (at most one a week, plus event reminders), a place in the WhatsApp community,
   certificates for events they attend, and a profile under **My club** once they sign in with GitHub.
5. The club keeps only what it needs: name, email, GitHub username, which events someone came to, and anything
   they chose to add. It is never sold or shared outside the club, except with the university when an event
   report asks for an attendance count.

## 4. The core team

The core team runs the club for one academic year (a **term**, July to June).

| Position | Looks after |
| --- | --- |
| President | The club's direction, the university, and making sure the rest of this charter is followed |
| Vice President | Standing in for the President, and the events calendar |
| Secretary | Meetings, minutes, the decisions register, and official documents |
| Design Lead | The club's look: posters, the site, merch |
| External Relations Head | Speakers, sponsors, other clubs and communities |
| Leads and members | One of the crews below, and the work given to them at meetings |

**To decide:** whether a Treasurer is needed once the club handles money (see section 8).

Alongside the positions, members of the core team take **technical roles** in three crews, with levels I, II and III:

- **Architecture & Strategy**: Git Custodian, Code Gatekeeper, Tech Scout.
- **Automation & Pipeline**: Pipeline Architect, Secrets & Security Officer.
- **Execution & Onboarding**: Issue Alchemist, Stack Explorer, Onboarding Forge.

The full description of each role is on the site's Team page. A level goes up when the person has done the work
of the next level for a term, agreed at a meeting.

**Mentors** are former core team members who stay on to advise. They have no vote, and can be given access to the
admin pages like anyone else.

## 5. Joining the core team

1. New core team members are chosen through a **recruitment round**, opened under `/admin` → **Recruitment** and
   announced on WhatsApp and Instagram. Rounds usually open at the start of a term.
2. Every application is read by **at least two reviewers**, and nobody reviews someone they are close to.
3. **Everyone who applies gets a reply by email**, whatever the answer.
4. A shortlist is agreed at a core team meeting and recorded in the decisions register.

**To decide:** whether the President is chosen by the outgoing core team, by a vote of the new core team, or by
the faculty coordinator with the team's recommendation.

## 6. Access to the club's systems

1. Access to the admin pages is given as an **appointment** with an end date: *volunteer* (event-day staff:
   attendance, check-in, the timer and quizzes), *core* (the club's day-to-day work) or *admin* (emails, access and
   policies). Every appointment ends by itself on its date; nobody keeps access by being forgotten.
2. There are always **at least two admins**. The Monday digest warns when there aren't.
3. Every account the club depends on (domain, hosting, database, GitHub, the club Gmail, Luma, social accounts) has
   an **owner and a different backup**, listed under **Assets & handover**. Passwords are never written there, only
   where they are kept.
4. At the end of a term, the outgoing admins hand over with the checklist under **Assets & handover**, in the order
   in [ROLLOVER.md](ROLLOVER.md), and give the new admins access **before** their own ends.

## 7. Meetings and decisions

1. The core team meets at least **every two weeks** during term. The Secretary writes the minutes in
   `/admin` → **Meetings & decisions** and finalises them within three days.
2. A meeting can make decisions when **more than half of the core team** is there.
3. Decisions are made by agreement where possible, and otherwise by a simple majority of those present. The
   President has a casting vote on a tie.
4. Every decision goes in the **decisions register**, with who decided and why. Action items get an owner and a date.
5. A decision that spends club money, changes this charter or removes someone needs a meeting, not a chat message.

## 8. Events and money

1. Every event starts as a **proposal** under `/admin` → **Event proposals**, and goes to the university's Student
   Life office for approval with the downloadable proposal document. Nothing is announced before it is approved.
2. After each event, the club writes a short recap and, where the university asks for one, an event report.
3. **Epoch Coins are not money.** They can't be bought for cash beyond the ticket, sold, or exchanged after the event.
4. Any real money (sponsorship, tickets, university funds) is recorded with a receipt, and every payment is approved
   by **two members of the core team**, one of them the President or the Treasurer.

**To decide:** where the club's money is held, and who can sign for it.

## 9. Conduct

1. Everyone at a club event or in a club space (online or in person) is welcome, whatever their background or
   experience. Harassment, discrimination and mocking beginners have no place here.
2. Anyone can report a problem to **any two members of the core team**, or to the club email. Reports are kept private.
3. The core team can ask someone to leave an event straight away. Removing someone from the club or the core team is
   decided at a meeting, after hearing from them, and recorded.
4. Code and content contributed to club projects follow the licence of that repository.

## 10. Changing this charter

1. A change is proposed in writing at least one week before a meeting.
2. It is agreed by **two thirds of the core team present**.
3. Two admins publish it as a new version under **Policies**. Old versions are kept, never edited.
